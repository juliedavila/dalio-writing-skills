# The evidence behind the rules

This is the short version of the research the skill is built on, for when the author asks "why?" or when a rule seems not to fit a situation. Strength is marked: **strong** (randomized field experiments, meta-analyses or replicated experiments on real readers), **experimental** (one or two controlled studies), **consensus** (the writing canon and institutional style guides agree, but nobody has tested it on readers) and **untested** (reasoned from related evidence). Most writing craft is consensus. That doesn't make it wrong, but it does mean it should bend to the situation.

## Readers

- **Shorter, simpler messages get more action (strong).** Cutting a 127-word email to 49 words raised responses from 2.7% to 4.8% among 7,002 school leaders; 67% of people predicted the opposite. Across five pooled field experiments with 866,104 recipients, shorter messages raised sign-ups, completions, donations and clicks. Rogers and Lasky-Fink, *Writing for Busy Readers* (2023); [HKS brief](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/HKSEE/HKSEE%20PDFs/Write%20shorter%20messages.pdf); [Lasky-Fink & Rogers 2023](https://doi.org/10.5465/amproc.2023.11540symposium). Caveat: tested on cold, low-stakes requests, not engaged colleagues.
- **Fewer asks get more action (strong).** Same body of work.
- **Bolding one part draws reading to it and away from the rest (book-reported).** [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/10/tips-on-how-to-connect-with-people-who-dont-have-time-to-read).
- **Simpler wording wins (strong).** Simpler headlines won across more than 30,000 A/B tests, and professional writers didn't share readers' preference. [Shulman et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn2555). Needlessly complex words lower readers' judgment of the writer's intelligence. [Oppenheimer 2006](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1178).
- **Jargon costs persuasion even when defined (experimental).** [Shulman et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0261927X20902177); [Bullock et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963662519865687). The penalty disappeared on an urgent topic, which fits incident communication.
- **Concrete language makes people feel listened to (experimental, field and lab).** [Packard & Berger 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaa038).
- **Probability words are read inconsistently, even by experts (consistent evidence).** [Beyth-Marom 1982](https://doi.org/10.1002/for.3980010305); [Willems et al. 2020](https://jcom.sissa.it/archive/19/02/JCOM_1902_2020_A03.pdf).
- **Formal letters drove more action than informal ones (strong).** Six studies, 67,632 people; comprehension didn't change. [Linos et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01761-z).
- **Hedge limits, not data (experimental).** Hedging data statements lowered evaluations, colloquial hedges most; honest limitations raised trust. [Durik et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1177/0261927X08317947); [Jensen 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2008.00324.x).
- **Emoji raise warmth and lower perceived competence (replicated).** [Lai & Mayiwar 2023](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.90195).
- **Trust drives preference far more than friendliness (experimental).** Trustworthiness explained about 52% of willingness to recommend; friendliness added about 8%. Humor lowered trust for a high-stakes product. Consumer copy, small effects. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/).
- **Stories and statistics tie overall; both together win (meta-analytic).** [Xu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/10410236.2022.2137750); [Allen et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1080/08824090009388781).
- **Suspected AI drafting lowers perceived sincerity and effort, mostly for personal messages; voice cues raise perceived authenticity (experimental).** [Zhu & Molnár 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15678); [Fan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790762).

## The canon

- **Actors as subjects, actions as verbs; old information before new; consistent topic strings; cut redundancy and announcing metadiscourse (consensus).** Joseph Williams, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*, read through [a UCF handout based on it](https://uwc.ucf.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2015/04/concision_checklist.pdf) and [a summary](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-joseph-m-williams-joseph-bizup/); Lanham's [Paramedic Method](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/paramedic_method.html).
- **The curse of knowledge, and its fixes: spell out abbreviations, give examples, test on a real reader, because empathy alone fails (consensus, with lab support for the bias).** [Pinker, "Why Academics Stink at Writing"](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/why_academics_stink_at_writing.pdf).
- **Orwell's six rules (consensus).** [Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/).
- **Point first, problem then solution then quantified benefits, objections answered (consensus).** Roman and Raphaelson, *Writing That Works*, via [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/writing-that-works-pdf-kenneth-roman-and-joel-raphaelson); Garner, *HBR Guide to Better Business Writing*, via [Bytepawn](https://bytepawn.com/hbr-guide-to-better-business-writing.html).
- **Plain language and 25-word sentence flag (consensus, weak numeric basis).** [GOV.UK](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/); [Inside GOV.UK on the 25-word limit](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2014/08/04/sentence-length-why-25-words-is-our-limit/).
- **Much popular grammar advice is folklore.** [Pullum, "50 Years of Stupid Grammar Advice"](https://pullum.ppls.ed.ac.uk/50years.pdf).
- **Voice stays constant, tone adapts (consensus).** [Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/); [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/fr-fr/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human); [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/voice-tone).

## How models write

- **Instruction-tuned models overuse present participial clauses (about 5x) and nominalizations (about 2x) compared with people; base models don't (strong).** [Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2).
- **Excess "style words" are measurable at scale (strong).** "Delves" 28x expected frequency in 2024 PubMed abstracts. [Kobak et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5); [Liang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07183).
- **Expert edits of AI prose: awkward wording 28%, sentence structure 20%, unnecessary exposition 18%, cliché 17%; mostly replacement, not deletion; no significant difference between GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Llama 3.1 (experimental).** [Chakrabarty et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509v5).
- **Slop is mostly about relevance, density and tone; model judges barely agree with humans on it (experimental).** [Shaib et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163v2).
- **Common signs of AI writing (practitioner catalog).** Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing", read via [a mirror](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Wikipedia%3ASigns_of_AI_writing). It says single signs are weak evidence.
- **Models can be too terse, and prompt style leaks into output (vendor guidance).** [Anthropic prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/de/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices); [OpenAI GPT-5.1 guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5-1_prompting_guide).
- **Writing to itself (untested).** No study measures undefined coined labels, unseen references or arrow shorthand in model output. The checks rest on Pinker's account of the curse of knowledge.

## Review and editing

- **Self-review is unreliable without outside structure (experimental).** Self-correction without feedback can make outputs worse ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)); self-refinement amplifies self-bias ([Xu et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.826.pdf)); models prefer their own outputs ([Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13076)); judges favor longer answers ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05685)).
- **Fixed, structured checklists help; generated ones often hurt (experimental).** Structured evaluation cut self-preference 31.5% ([Yang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22891)); about 40% of model-generated checklist items were harmful ([checklist study](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15218v1)).
- **Model edits scatter and shift meaning; human editors make self-contained edits (experimental).** [Ziegenbein et al., ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1789/).
- **Do no harm; query meaning, fix mechanics (professional practice).** [Saller, CMOS Shop Talk](https://cmosshoptalk.com/2015/11/30/editors-corner-4-2/); [ACES on author queries](https://aceseditors.org/news/2019/the-3-essential-elements-of-author-queries); [Milne, the art of editorial conversation](https://milnepublishing.geneseo.edu/processes/chapter/the-art-of-editorial-conversation/); [EFA editing levels](https://www.the-efa.org/editorial-services-definitions/); [UVA reverse outlining](https://writingcenter.virginia.edu/reverse-outlining-organization-strategy).
- **AI assistance homogenizes voice and lowers ownership; post-edited AI drafts stay closer to AI style than people think (experimental).** [Padmakumar & He](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05196v3); [Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11360); [Dhillon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11723); [Baumler et al., ACL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24444).
- **Style imitation: two to five same-genre samples are enough; topic-matched samples hurt; continuing the author's own opening matches best (experimental).** [Wang et al., EMNLP Findings 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1).

## Known gaps

No controlled study compares critique-then-revise with a direct rewrite for prose quality. No study tests banned-phrase lists in prompts. Nothing verified covers how writing should differ upward, across and down. Several canon sources were read through summaries rather than the books. Treat the skill's judgments in these areas as reasoned defaults, and let the author's accepted and rejected edits correct them over time.
